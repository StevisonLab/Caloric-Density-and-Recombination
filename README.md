# Caloric-Density-and-Recombination
Reproductive fitness does not predict plastic changes in recombination due to diet in _Drosophila melanogaster_

## Abstract

Meiotic recombination is a fundamental biological process that ensures proper chromosome segregation and generates novel genetic variation. Recombination rate exhibits plasticity in response to environmental conditions, yet how genetic background interacts with nutrition to influence this plasticity remains poorly understood. Using _Drosophila melanogaster_ lines from the Drosophila Genetic Reference Panel (DGRP), we evaluated how varying caloric density affects X-chromosome recombination, body mass, fecundity, and survival across genotypes and over time. Caloric restriction significantly reduced female body mass in both genotypes, indicating a shared physiological response to dietary manipulation. Dietary treatment did not significantly affect egg-to-adult survival in marker-free crosses, whereas the high-calorie diet significantly reduced survival in marker-present crosses. After accounting for differential viability, we detected a significant genotype-by-environment (G×E) interaction in X-chromosome recombination rates. Furthermore, shifts in recombination rate were not associated with reduced survival or fecundity, failing to support adaptive hypotheses as explanations for recombination plasticity. To assess overall ovarian gene expression during these dietary perturbations, we conducted ovarian transcriptomic profiling (RNA-seq). Ovarian gene expression analysis revealed distinct, genotype-specific transcriptional responses to caloric restriction and excess calories, including significant changes in metabolic, stress-response, and cellular maintenance pathways across early and late time points. Overall, these findings demonstrate genotype-dependent recombination plasticity in response to dietary variation, without a consistent relationship between recombination rate and measured components of reproductive fitness, providing no support for the prediction of fitness-associated recombination under the dietary conditions examined. This work highlights the complex relationship of recombination rate plasticity among genotype, environment, and reproductive traits.
